Why this structure changes the game

1. Crystallizes the real decision

Most tools start with vendor comparison. DecisionBrief starts earlier: what triggered this, what does success look like, what are you actually deciding between — including do-nothing. That executive summary alone saves hours of misdirected research.

2. Surfaces tradeoffs & hidden constraints

Budget < $100/mo, no contracts, 24/7 chat+voice, 99% accuracy bar — in the sample, tradeoffs become explicit: higher cost may improve accuracy but break budget, CRM adds complexity but helps data, short trial delays deployment.

3. Risks, unknowns, decision criteria

Unlike generic AI, the brief separates risks (hallucinations, privacy, integration), unknowns (exact cost, accuracy metrics, trial terms), and concrete criteria you will use to judge options. You walk into vendor calls with a checklist, not vibes.

4. Actionable next steps, not just summary

1) Identify 3–5 candidates that fit budget, 2) Request 1–2 month trial, 3) Assess spreadsheet integration, 4) Compare trial vs criteria, 5) Implement + monitor. A recommendation with a test path, not a forced purchase.
